Deep in the jungles of Campeche, Mexico a few hours drive from the city of Campeche is one of the great mayan sites - Calakmul.

Calakmul was one of the rivals of Tikal, which is located in Guatemala.

This site is massive in scale. As at many mayan sites, the pyramids have been cleared of vegetation and heavily restored. This is what accounts for the sharp angles of the bricks.

Unlike sites such as Copan, the condition of the stellae at Calakmul is poor - for two reasons: weather erosion, and the activities of archaelogists, who sawed off the faces of some stellae and transported them back to American museums. 

Unlike sites such as Chichen Itza, it is permitted to walk up the restored pyramids - and many visitos are irrestibly drawn to do so. Ascending these enormous structures brings one to the top of the jungle canopy and affords impressive views far into the distance.

A number of spectacular jade funerary masks excavated from Calakmul are on display at Campeche's archaeological museum, which is itself housed within a very interesting spanish fort.
