One of my long-standing travel goals had been to to visit aome of the classic old american cities in the South. 

In 2016, I had the opportuntity to visit two of these: Savannah and also, on a short side-trip, Charleston.

This was my first trip to the American South, and I was really interested to learn more about the culture and history of its old cities first hand.

Prior to the Civil War, the US South had not seen anything like the urbanisation seen in parts of the North. Throughout the South, only a few cities had emerged: Savannah, Charleston, Nashville, New Orleans, Memphis, etc. Much of the South was agricultural, with much land owned by large wealthy landowners and worked by enslaved populations. There was also a significant amount of small land-holding. Cities like Savannah and Charleston became famous for their osentatious mansions, some of which continue to be maintained as interesting house museums.

Savannah is also a rare example of a planned American city. It was founded with a network of squares surrounded by churches, mansions and other impressive buildings - a scheme that has been preserved to the present.

Today, it is fascinating to walk around the historic area of the City. Impressive antebellum mansions stand beside many other interesting buildings with architectural details such as outstanding cast-iron construction.

Savannah also has a glorious major park close to its downtown: Forsyth Park, which features a distinctive cast-iron fountain.

One thing I had't known about was the Irish history in Savannah. Apparently, a significant number of Irish people from the North would regularly travel there for seasonal work.
