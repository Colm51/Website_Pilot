I have visited Amsterdam a number of times over the past 10 years.

The first thing that obviously strikes the visitor are the canals - which form concentric part-circles radiating out from the centre. And alongside the canals are the famous Dutch canal houses, with their distinctive tall and narrow profile.

Many of the canals are the product of an energetic period of building and planning in the early 1600s. Its sometimes said that the narrow canal buildings resulted from tax reigmes such as taxes on frontage. In reality, the civic authorities regulated the size of lots. The wealthiest families could build on two or more lots, but the characteristic profile of buildings was heavily influenced by an early example of land-use regulations.

The wealthiest families could certainly have afforded taxes on frontage. Their taste for large palazzos is revealed in places such as Maarssen, where they built lavish summer retreats.
