Towards the end of a trip to the Netherlands, I spent a few days in Naples, including a side trip to Pompeii. 

Naples is an overwhelming, vibrant, city. It is nothing like Florence, which I had visited last year.

My first impression of Naples, arriving on a bus from the airport, was of an opera set. Hulking apartment blocks with laundry drying on balconies. 

Naples is situated on the side of a mountain, and much of the city is built on its slopes. My first accommodation was located a few subway stops from the central station, and it was an amazing adventure navigating the narrow, winding streets to my destination.

The historic centre of the City was once a place of lavish palazzos and ornate churches and other religious buildings. The buildings remain, but the palazzos have long since been divided into apartments or businesses. In this regard, there are similarities to the vecindades of Mexico City.

The historic centre is a surreal place. Laundry hangs overhead, scooters are everywhere, and behind the facades of churches one finds astounding interiors replete with marble. Remnants of Roman buildings are incorporated into later buildings, and under the street are the remains of older roman settlements.

Naples has a very long and extraordinarily complex history. One period that particularly intrigued me was the period that it was under Spanish Habsburg rule. During the period that also saw the Dutch golden age, Naples was one of the largest cities in Europe, and a major cultural centre. This is a city where one can see a Caravaggio in situ in the altar where it was commissioned, and where you see a testimonial to Vico on the side of a building.

Its also a city where you can visit a crypt where a skull with a wedding veil is still maintained as part of an obscure practice related to puragtory. And where relics are piled warehouse - style high in a cathedral.

It was impossible to see even a fraction of the attractions in Naples, especially as nearby attractions also beckoned.

Pompeii is reasonably short train ride from Naples, and the site has its own train stop. I devoted half a day to visiting this site.

The site of Pompeii is enmormous - not surprisingly, as it an entire ruined city that stretches block after block. Its remarkable to think that the entire site was covered with debris at one point.

In fact, its possible to get a bit lost, as signage is limited, and many parts of the site do not have any tourists walking about. A number of the structures have been restored, and while signage is limited, following the various guided tours reliably led me to some of the more well-known buildings.
