Having been to Savannah and Charleston, the next southern city I visited was New Orleans.

New Orleans is a very different city than these two - much more vibrant, gritty and lively. New Orleans has an incredible local music scene, and I checked out a number of superb jazz performances in the Treme and on Frenchman St. 

In the New Orleans context, Jazz often means an upbeat, dancable form of music with lively brass instruments and plenty of raucus audience participation. I saw one excellent sit down modern jazz performance, but the rest of the shows I saw were lively.

I stayed in the Gardden District, which was established as a protestant, american area following the acquisition of the city from the french in the early 19 century. This is an extremely picturesque area, with many exquisite, distinctive homes / mansions.

The French Quarter was fascinating. This area was actually developed by the Spanish, but was associated the french elite. This area is full of exquisite buildings and much superb cast iron work.

The food in New Orleans was also predictably excellent, and I had lots of fun trying out oyster Po' Boys, Muffaletta sandwiches, rice and beans, and other classic dishes.

I didn't explore the famous cemetaries in the French Quarter, which at that time were accesible only with tours. I did, however, explore other cemetaries, including one in the Garden District. These also featured the famous New Orleans tombs. There have been various theories as to why New Orleans has such distintive tombs. Subsequently, during my travels in latin america, I noticed that such tombs are actually common throughout former spanish colonies.

Just outside the French Quarter is the storied Treme neighbourhood, a historic black neighbourhood with a rich tradition of music. I enjoyed a night of jazz at a club there, which featured one of the locally-famous Andrew brothers, and where I met some colourful characters.

I also took a tour of a nearby bayou, which was full of atmosphere with its brackish water and spanish moss. Along the bayou there was a very evocative old plantation cemetary build on top of an old Indian mound.
