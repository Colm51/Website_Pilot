In 2018 I visited the historic New Mexico city of Santa Fe. This area of the US is home to even more historicm still-standing indigenous Pueblo settlements, and was the northern extent of the Spanish colonial presence. As such, it long predates the founding of the US, and is still home to many people who can trace their ancestory back to the period before the founding.

Santa Fe became known as an artists' location during the generation of Georgia O'Keefe, and is also well-know for the nearby town of Taos, which is home to a pueblo.

The local architecture developed a distinct adobe form. There are a number of existing very striking churches that are still re-finished regularly in adobe by the local community.

The distinct form of adobe structures has been maintained in more modern structures - flat roofs, square forms, etc, but many of the more modern buildings are not actually adobe. Santa Fe has maintained a distinctive look as a result of strict development rules.

The old adobe structures are fascinating - and the interior of the colonial era churches are much simpler than the extravagent structures found throughout present-day Mexico. On the frontier of the colonial possessions, there was not the same access to valuable materials. However, the results are fascinating in their own right.

This was also one of the areas of the classic cowboys - before the american cowboys, there were the vaqueros, and after the US civil war, personalities such as Billy the Kid were active in this area.

The racial mix of the hispanic and indigenous inhabitants is very complex. This was a violent area, with slaving raids by both the spanish and indigenous groups such as Comanche, and recent research has confirmed that many residents have a mix of spanish and local indigenous heritage, even if this may have been long-forgotten. 

This area was also the site of one of the few succesful uprisings against the spanish in the colonial era.

A fascinating part of America.
